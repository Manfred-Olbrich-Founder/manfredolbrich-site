problem:
  heading: "Teaching online meant fighting the tools."
  lead: "In lockdown, tutoring moved online overnight. The tools it moved onto were built for meetings, not lessons."
  points:
    - title: "Nothing fitted together"
      text: "Tutors juggled a video call, a separate whiteboard, a payment app and email. None of it was designed for teaching."
    - title: "Every subject needs its own tools"
      text: "A maths tutor needs to draw a function and its graph. A music teacher needs to write notes and hear them played. No classroom on the market did both."
    - title: "The open-source starting point had hard limits"
      text: "We built on BigBlueButton, an open-source classroom. It handled video and a basic whiteboard, and almost nothing else we needed."
steps:
  label: "What we built"
  heading: "Five problems, solved one at a time."
  items:
    - title: "A whiteboard that behaves"
      text: "Drag in a document, image or video, then move, resize and draw on it. It lands in the right place even while you scroll."
    - title: "Maths and music tools"
      text: "Type a function and get a graph you can draw on. Write chords and triplets and hear them on every student's computer."
    - title: "Servers that scale ahead of demand"
      text: "Extra servers start before the evening peak, not after lessons begin to stutter."
    - title: "Recordings with everything in them"
      text: "Screen, voice and every whiteboard annotation, merged into one video for revision and for resolving disputes."
    - title: "Works on an iPad"
      text: "Many tutors taught on iPads, so the whole whiteboard had to work by touch."
decisions:
  heading: "Six calls I made, and why."
  items:
    - title: "Don't make tutors write like programmers"
      text: "Graphs failed when a tutor typed a function the normal way, such as e^x. Instead of teaching tutors a strict syntax, we built a translator that turns everyday maths notation into what the graphing engine expects."
    - title: "Scale on the timetable, not on the panic"
      text: "We added Scalelite to balance load across servers, but on its own it added capacity too slowly for a sudden spike. So we wrote scripts that read the lesson timetable and the number of students booked, and started new AWS servers before peak hours arrived."
    - title: "Ship the simple version, then replace it"
      text: "Full video recording was not yet possible, so we first gave tutors a PDF of the whiteboard after each lesson. When recording became possible, it replaced the PDF."
    - title: "Recording had to capture the whiteboard, not just the faces"
      text: "Recordings were for students to revise and for us to settle disagreements fairly. The standard recording tool kept only 7 of our 25 whiteboard annotation types, which made it useless for both. That requirement drove us to build our own."
    - title: "Private lessons stay private"
      text: "We let visitors drop into live lessons to see the classroom at work. When a user pointed out that nobody wants a stranger appearing in a private session, I asked for it to be limited to public courses."
    - title: "Try, measure, change course"
      text: "Recording took three failed approaches before the fourth worked. Each one was tested properly and dropped for a clear reason: missing annotations, a missing shared screen, then too much strain on the tutor's computer."
extra:
  label: "The hard parts"
  heading: "Where the real work was."
  intro: "None of these had a ready-made answer."
  items:
    - title: "The graph that crashed the board"
      text: "Plotting a function froze the whole whiteboard. We traced it to how the points were drawn and stored, and one change of method fixed it."
    - title: "Our own coordinate grid"
      text: "No grid library let a tutor pick their own axes, so we built one, including polar coordinates."
    - title: "Safari blocked the music"
      text: "Safari will not play sound without permission. Safari users get a one-time button, without slowing the tool down for everyone else."
    - title: "One video, many sources"
      text: "Screen, audio and annotations had to merge into one file small enough to store cheaply and upload quickly. We tuned the quality settings to find the balance."
    - title: "Touch in a mouse world"
      text: "The third-party libraries assumed a mouse. We rewrote parts of them so drag-and-drop works with a finger."
    - title: "A map of the board"
      text: "A small live map of the whole whiteboard, so a tutor can jump to any section. We found no library for it, so it was written from scratch."
