problem:
  heading: "Nobody could answer a simple question."
  lead: "“How many lessons do I have left?” A parent could not see it, and we could not answer without digging."
  points:
    - title: "Parents saw payments, not lessons"
      text: "Their account showed a ledger of what they had paid. It did not show how many lessons that bought, or how many were left in a package."
    - title: "Reconciliation was detective work"
      text: "Some lessons had taken place off the platform. Matching each customer payment to the right tutor invoice meant piecing the record together by hand."
    - title: "Every account was set up by hand"
      text: "After a sale, someone created the family's account manually. That caused delays, wrong lesson counts and mistakes in tutor pay, and account managers tracked credits in spreadsheets."
steps:
  label: "How it works"
  heading: "One payment sets everything in motion."
  items:
    - title: "A parent pays for a plan"
      text: "The signed deal in the CRM carries the plan, the learners and the tutor."
    - title: "The account builds itself"
      text: "Credits, permissions and tutor rates are created automatically. Target: within five minutes."
    - title: "A weekly schedule appears"
      text: "Recurring lessons are generated from the plan, on a rolling window of six to eight weeks."
    - title: "A lesson is checked"
      text: "Three signals confirm it really happened before any credit is used."
    - title: "Three records update at once"
      text: "The credit is used, the parent is told, and the lesson lands on the tutor's invoice."
decisions:
  heading: "Five calls I made, and why."
  items:
    - title: "Sell a weekly rhythm, not a pile of credits"
      text: "Families think “maths every Tuesday at five”, not “four credits a month”. So the plan is shown as a weekly rhythm, and the credit maths runs underneath."
    - title: "The schedule leads, the ledger follows"
      text: "Billing is monthly, but some months have five Tuesdays. I ruled that the system may go one lesson into the negative and settle it at the next payment. The parent never sees a warning, and the child never misses a lesson over an accounting quirk."
    - title: "A lesson counts only when three signals agree"
      text: "It lasted at least 20 minutes, the assigned tutor was present, and the student spoke, confirmed from the transcript. If any signal is missing, the lesson is flagged for a person to decide. No credit is taken on a guess."
    - title: "One event, one source of truth"
      text: "A confirmed lesson updates the family's credits and the tutor's invoice in the same step. At month end each tutor gets a single invoice, and the bulk payment file must match the invoices to the penny."
    - title: "Nothing is ever deleted"
      text: "The ledger only grows. A mistake is reversed with a logged reason, never erased, so every balance can be explained to a parent or an auditor."
extra:
  label: "Real life"
  heading: "The edge cases are the product."
  intro: "A subscription is easy on paper. I wrote a rule for each way a real family breaks it."
  items:
    - title: "A child is ill"
      text: "Skip or move the lesson. The credit is kept and the weekly rhythm carries on."
    - title: "Exams are coming"
      text: "Add extra lessons for a fixed period, without changing the whole plan."
    - title: "Extras become a habit"
      text: "Two months of add-ons in a row prompts the account manager to offer a better-value plan."
    - title: "A payment fails"
      text: "Upcoming lessons are held. A person can grant a grace period; the system never does it alone."
    - title: "Credits go unused"
      text: "They roll over for two billing cycles, and the parent is warned a week before any expire."
    - title: "A family leaves"
      text: "The notice month squares the account: lessons taken against lessons paid, so it closes fairly."

      text: "Every customer payment ties to a lesson and every lesson ties to a tutor invoice. No more detective work."
